Demonstrations.

Teacher Replay executes a fixed plan without object feedback. It cannot correct contact-induced deviations and may leave the target ungraspable. The online teacher achieves the highest hardware success, but repeatedly retracts the arm to recover complete scene state. P M B S, the previous state of the art, combines tree search with batched simulation; planning and scene reacquisition increase end to end time. Spiral is a target-centric pushing heuristic. It requires an updated target pose at each decision and retracts the arm when the target is occluded. Spiral is fastest on average, but unreliable in these tight workspaces, with lower success than TRACE and a substantial rate of out of workspace failures.

TRACE uses one privileged rollout to guide feedback control under self occlusion. A complete initial observation initializes the digital twin, and the teacher produces a nominal plan before manipulation begins. During pushing, the recurrent student combines partial object observations, robot proprioception, memory, and local rollout context to select its next action. The nominal plan stays fixed, while the executed actions adapt to the observed scene. As contact moves objects away from their predicted positions, the controller can depart from the plan. The observation history records object visibility and the age of the last observation, retaining context when the arm blocks the view. No online teacher queries, simulator rollouts, or sensing retractions are required during pushing. The rollout defines the scene-specific execution horizon. The arm withdraws for the final graspability check before retrieval. This separates closed-loop pushing under partial visibility from the final check that requires a clear view.

Across the hardware benchmark, TRACE achieves ninety percent success, with no observed workspace violations or sensing retractions during pushing. Its remaining failures reach the execution budget. TRACE is more reliable than replay and Spiral, and improves both success and total time over P M B S. Spiral remains faster on average. The online teacher reaches ninety five percent success; TRACE is two point nine times faster end to end. One initial plan supports adaptive execution without repeated complete-scene reacquisition.

RESEARCH OVERVIEW

Abstract

One initial prediction.
Feedback through self-occlusion.

Read the full manuscript ↗

Retrieving objects from dense clutter requires rearrangement during which the manipulator can occlude objects while moving them. Repeated arm withdrawals to restore visibility interrupt execution. We introduce TRACE, a plan-conditioned imitation framework for retrieval under self-occlusion.

A single unoccluded observation initializes a digital twin, where a privileged teacher generates a fixed nominal rollout. A recurrent student combines local rollout context, partial object observations, and proprioception to select actions that can correct deviations from the prediction. Behavior cloning initializes the student; DAgger refines it with teacher labels on student-visited states. The rollout remains fixed throughout execution, so the deployed student needs neither online teacher queries nor additional simulator rollouts during pushing.

On 511 simulation test scenes, TRACE achieves 90.7% success versus 43.4% for nominal replay and 96.7% for the privileged closed-loop teacher. At a matched 26,373-label budget, student-state supervision achieves 87.8% versus 66.7% for expert-only cloning, demonstrating gains beyond additional labels. On a UR5e, TRACE achieves 90.0% success versus 95.0% for the closed-loop teacher, while reducing total execution time from 192.7 s to 67.3 s. It avoids the teacher’s 16.8 sensing-related arm retractions per trial during pushing, retaining a final withdrawal for graspability evaluation.

01 THE PROBLEM & HARDWARE

Moving the clutter
can hide the clutter.

The target is visible. The grasp is blocked. And the robot’s own arm gets in the way of seeing what moves next.

The task is to create enough clearance for a top-down grasp of a designated object, then grasp and lift it while keeping all objects inside the workspace. TRACE studies dense planar clutter with known object footprints and a complete initial scene observation.

Each push changes object contacts and grasping clearance. The arm and gripper can hide objects that continue moving, so the initial prediction can become inaccurate. Withdrawing the arm recovers a clear view, but interrupts manipulation.

02 RELATED WORK

Where TRACE fits.

The work connects retrieval through rearrangement, predictive planning, and privileged imitation learning. The key question is how to keep useful feedback when the manipulator hides a changing scene.

01 / RETRIEVAL THROUGH REARRANGEMENT

Creating room for a grasp.

Push–grasp methods learn to move clutter to improve grasp access. Mechanical Search studies multi-step retrieval of targets hidden by clutter; Visuomotor Mechanical Search learns closed-loop retrieval. Kiatos et al. also study sequential pre-grasp pushes without retracting for every observation.

TRACE’s focus: the scene is initially visible, then the manipulator creates occlusion during execution. The contribution combines rollout context, recurrent feedback, and explicit missing-object observations; uninterrupted pushing alone is not the distinction.

02 / PREDICTIVE PLANNING

Using a prediction during contact.

Visual Foresight Trees searches over outcomes from a learned interaction model. MORE interleaves tree search with self-supervised learning, while PMBS accelerates long-horizon search through batched rigid-body simulations.

TRACE’s use of prediction: a single teacher rollout is generated before manipulation. Its predicted geometry becomes context for the student’s action decisions. During pushing, the rollout remains fixed and the student acts without further search or simulator rollouts.

03 / PRIVILEGED TEACHERS & MEMORY

Learning with more than you can see.

Learning by Cheating trains a sensorimotor student from a teacher with privileged state. Mosbach and Behnke use memory-augmented student–teacher learning for retrieval from imperfect detections. These approaches motivate learning useful behavior from richer training-time information.

TRACE’s information split: complete state provides teacher supervision in simulation and a pre-execution rollout. The deployed student combines that rollout with partial object observations, robot state, and recurrent memory; it does not query the teacher during pushing.

04 / LEARNER STATES & NOMINAL GUIDANCE

Learning how to depart from a plan.

DAgger collects expert labels on states induced by the learner, addressing the shift away from expert demonstrations. Residual Reinforcement Learning adds a learned correction to a conventional controller’s output.

TRACE’s action formulation: the student receives geometric rollout context and predicts the complete next motion primitive. Matched-label experiments isolate the benefit of supervision on student-visited states. The nominal trajectory guides the policy without restricting actions to a nominal command plus a residual.

Selected connections from the manuscript’s Related Work section. Linked papers describe the original methods; quantitative comparisons on TRACE’s benchmark appear in Results.

Inside TRACE

Read the inference diagram from A → B → C → D: build the reference once, encode what is visible now, update the recurrent policy, and execute the next action.

A

A fixed nominal rollout

The initial scene estimate initializes the digital twin. The frozen teacher predicts end-effector positions and object centers. The student reads a K = 4 window of end-effector waypoints, predicted object centers at the current nominal step, rollout progress, and a plan-exhaustion indicator.

Positions are expressed relative to the robot’s current measured end-effector position. This local context changes as the robot moves, even though the stored rollout does not.

B

Explicitly missing observations

Each tracked object has a token containing its visible geometry, a visibility flag, observation age, and target identity. When an object is unavailable, its geometry is zeroed while those indicators remain encoded.

A shared per-object multilayer perceptron (MLP), followed by mean and max pooling, produces a scene representation that is invariant to object ordering. Teacher and student use separate encoder parameters.

C

A recurrent action policy

A fusion MLP combines the scene representation, local plan context, end-effector position and boundary distances, and previous action. A gated recurrent unit (GRU) carries observation–action history across missing detections.

A categorical head selects one of 16 planar motion primitives: cardinal, diagonal, or two-segment staircase motions. The GRU is trained for action selection, without an explicit hidden-scene reconstruction objective.

D

Close the loop, then check

The robot executes the selected primitive and receives another partial observation. The student can change the direction of execution as the real scene departs from the prediction.

Pushing ends at the scene-specific teacher-relative step/travel budget; there is no learned stop action. The arm then withdraws for graspability evaluation and retrieval. No teacher query or fresh simulator rollout is used during pushing.

What stays fixed?The nominal rollout.What adapts?Partial observations, recurrent state, local relative context, and the next action.

The student learns in simulation from a frozen teacher. The teacher sees complete geometric state; the student’s inputs are restricted to the observations and rollout context available at deployment.

01 / PRIVILEGED TEACHER

Learn with complete state.

A recurrent teacher is trained with proximal policy optimization (PPO), using complete task geometry and a graspability-based reward. Once trained, its parameters remain frozen while it supplies nominal rollouts and action-distribution labels.

02 / BEHAVIOR CLONING

Initialize from demonstrations.

Teacher-controlled trajectories pair the student’s partial inputs with the teacher’s distribution over actions. Simulated arm occlusion, object-token dropout, and multi-decision blackouts expose the student to missing observations.

03 / DATASET AGGREGATION

Supervise student-visited states.

During DAgger, the student controls execution and the frozen teacher labels the states it reaches. The new sequences are added to the dataset and the next student is fitted on the aggregate. Teacher labels supervise training; they do not replace the student’s executed action.

Training objective. A recovery-weighted forward-KL loss matches the full teacher action distribution. Occluded observations remain eligible for supervision. Held-out validation scenes select the deployed student; matched-label results distinguish student-state coverage from simply collecting more labels.

How the evaluation is set up+

Separate training, validation, and test scenes.

The simulation benchmark uses 2,097 training scenes, 178 held-out validation scenes, and 511 independently generated test scenes. Validation selects models; the test set is reserved for final evaluation.

On hardware, success requires the target to become graspable, be grasped and lifted, and satisfy workspace constraints. The setup uses known object footprints in dense planar clutter.

06 FAILURE ANALYSIS

Where TRACE still falls short.

Scene 05 exposes a remaining gap: Online Teacher retrieves the target, while TRACE ends without a viable grasp. Both complete recordings are shown here, separately from the main galleries.

What this example motivates

In the TRACE recording, the digital-twin rollout is marked graspable, but the final real-scene check finds no viable grasp. This motivates studying when to acquire a new observation or refresh a fixed nominal rollout. These recordings show an outcome gap; they do not isolate which component caused the failure.

07 SCOPE & OPEN QUESTIONS

What this work
does—and leaves open.

A defined setting

Evidence covers dense planar clutter, known object footprints, and one initially complete scene observation. Unfamiliar geometry and uncertain initial estimates need further evaluation.

A fixed reference has limits

TRACE does not refresh the nominal plan during pushing. Detecting when that plan becomes uninformative—and deciding when to acquire another view—is an open direction.

Execution ends at a budget

The policy selects discrete motion primitives and has no learned stop action. Continuous actions, learned termination, and non-planar 3D clutter are future work.
